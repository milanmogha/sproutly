The LinkedIn post

A launch post for a product that's yours — no client to clear it with. The first two lines are what shows before "…more", so they carry the hook alone.

in Milan MoghaFounder, Duforge · We forge software

It's 8:14 PM and a parent wants last month's fee receipt. The school checks the WhatsApp group. Then the paper register. Then fees_final_v3.xlsx. Three places, and none of them agree. So we built the thing that replaces all three. It's called Sproutly. One app, four surfaces, built and run by us at Duforge: → Parents see the day as it happens — meals, nap, mood, health, attendance, photos. Nobody has to ask at pickup. → Teachers mark the register, write the daily diary and raise a PTM request from their phone, between classes. → The school office runs admissions, staff, communication and fees end to end: fee structure → invoices → payment → receipt. Every payment gets its own receipt, so ₹12,000 in cash and ₹8,000 by UPI against one invoice produce two receipts, not a note in the margin. → And a platform console on our side, where a school is onboarded in minutes and a three-branch group is grouped under one customer — while every branch stays a separate tenant with its own login, its own data and its own subscription. Three decisions I'd defend in any review: 1. Money has two directions and we never mix them. Fees go into the school's own Razorpay account and we never touch them. The subscription is the only money that reaches us. 2. An owner is a grouping record, never an account. Three branches under one trust sit side by side on our console, but no branch can read a sibling's data — scopeFilter stays keyed on the school, under every query. 3. The school calls a PTM; the parent is told. A parent never requests or accepts a meeting slot, and both routes exclude them on the server, not just in the UI. It sounds like a small product decision. It's the difference between a calendar and one more group chat. There are no fixed plans, either. A subscription is billed per invoice for any number of days, so a 15-day trial and a two-year contract are the same shape in the code. Flutter on mobile, NestJS and MongoDB behind it, shipped from a self-hosted runner. If you run a preschool — or three — I'll walk you through it in 10 minutes. Comment "demo" or send me a DM. #SaaS #EdTech #Preschool #EarlyChildhoodEducation #Flutter #NestJS #BuildInPublic

Getting the MP4

Turning this into a video file

The reel plays in the browser; LinkedIn needs a file. Screen-record it once — Windows has a recorder built in.

STEP 01

Pick the ratio

1:1 fills the most feed space on desktop and mobile both. Use 4:5 for maximum mobile height, 9:16 only if you'll also cut it for Reels.

STEP 02

Enter record mode

Everything but the reel disappears and it fills the screen. Esc exits, Space replays.

STEP 03

Capture

Press Win+Alt+R for Xbox Game Bar, or Win+Shift+R for Snipping Tool. Start the recorder first, then press Space.

STEP 04

Trim and post

Crop to the reel, trim the dead ends, export MP4. 36 seconds sits well inside LinkedIn's 10-minute limit. Post it silent — most of the feed is muted anyway.
